Trip prep for the Transylvania County trout country around **Brevard, NC** — the self-styled "Land of Waterfalls" — plus everything a short drive east and west: **DuPont State Recreational Forest** (east, toward Hendersonville), **Rosman and the French Broad headwaters** (west), and the higher **Cashiers / Highlands plateau** (southwest). This is the densest concentration of public trout water in the Southern Appalachians, and you can fish a technical tailwater-style spring creek, a stocked delayed-harvest run, and a native brook-trout headwater all in the same day.

> **The headline: two things dictate this trip — the delayed-harvest calendar and the state of Helene recovery.**
>
> **1. Delayed Harvest (DH) calendar.** NC's DH waters (French Broad East Fork, DuPont's Little River, the Tuckasegee, and others) fish *catch-and-release, single-hook artificial-only* from **Oct 1 through the first Saturday in June**. That window is the whole point — heavy fall/winter/spring stockings, no harvest, and the best numbers of the year (50-fish days on the Tuck are real). From the first Saturday in June through Sept 30 those same waters flip to hatchery-supported harvest regs, get hammered, and thin out. **If you have any say over dates, come in the DH window.**
>
> **2. Hurricane Helene (Sept 2024) recovery.** The storm reshaped these watersheds — sedimentation, blown-out access roads, bridge replacements, and shifted stocking points. As of 2026, most water is fishable but **access is the live variable**: e.g., Davidson River Rd (FS 475) has had segments closed for bridge work, and NCWRC has moved some stocking locations. **Re-check the [Pisgah Ranger District closures page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/northcarolina/alerts) and [ncwildlife.gov/trout](https://www.ncwildlife.gov/trout) in the days before you go** — that's the one thing that changes this plan.

## The strategy

- **Match the water type to the day, not the other way around.** Three distinct games live within 45 minutes of Brevard: technical wild/C&R water (Davidson), numbers-y stocked DH runs (East Fork, Little River, Tuck), and small-stream native brookies (high plateau headwaters).
- **Davidson is the technical anchor** — it fishes clear and cold year-round below the Setzer hatchery, holds big, pellet-conditioned, famously picky trout, and rewards small flies and long leaders. Treat it like a spring creek.
- **DH runs are the confidence water** in the Oct–June window — go here when you want bent rods and a guide-friendly day.
- **Native brook trout are the reward** — the high, cold headwaters of the Highlands/Cashiers plateau and upper Pisgah hold the South's only native trout ("specks"). Small water, dry-dropper, bluebird afternoons.
- **Southern Appalachian fly boxes are their own dialect** — the [Yallarhammer, Thunderhead, Green Weenie, and Tellico Nymph](https://gardenandgun.com/articles/five-classic-southern-trout-flies/) are regional standards a local guide will actually reach for. Carry a few even if your Western boxes cover the rest.

## Brevard core: the Davidson & upper Pisgah

### Davidson River (Pisgah National Forest) — technical anchor

Regularly ranked among the top 100 trout streams in the country, and the reason people drive here. The **wild, catch-and-release, fly-fishing-only water runs from the headwaters down to Avery Creek**; the stretch right below the **Bobby N. Setzer State Fish Hatchery** (the state's largest) holds outsized, pellet-conditioned rainbows and browns that have seen every fly in the shop. Clear, cold, and technical — this is a small-flies-and-long-leaders game.

- Midges: Zebra Midge (black/red), WD-40, disco midge (#18–22)
- Sulphurs (#16–18) — the signature late-spring/early-summer hatch
- Blue-Winged Olive dry/emerger (#18–20) — overcast days
- Hendrickson, Quill Gordon, March Brown (#12–16) — the early-spring parade
- Pheasant Tail / Hare's Ear nymphs (#16–20)
- Below the hatchery only: San Juan worm, egg pattern, mop fly — the pellet-fed fish key on them
- 5x–6x tippet; downsize before you think you need to

### Looking Glass Creek & Avery Creek (Pisgah tributaries) — backups

Small, roadside, easy-access water off US-276 for an evening session. Looking Glass Falls and the Sliding Rock natural waterslide are right here, so this doubles as sightseeing water. Wild and stocked rainbows; freestone conditions.

- Yellow Stimulator / Yellow Sally (#12–16)
- Elk Hair Caddis, tan/olive (#14–18)
- Parachute Adams (#14–18)
- Hopper-dropper: small foam terrestrial over a beadhead nymph

## East: DuPont State Recreational Forest

10,000+ acres straddling Transylvania and Henderson counties, best known for its waterfalls (**Hooker, Triple, High, and Bridal Veil Falls** — the *Hunger Games* and *Last of the Mohicans* filming locations). The **Little River, from the Lake Dense outflow down to Hooker Falls, is delayed-harvest water** (C&R Oct–May, harvest June–Sept). Five lakes in the forest are inland public fishing waters — **Cascade Lake** and Lake Julia among them, worth a bank/float session for bass and stocked trout.

- DH stockers: Y2K/egg, Squirmy worm, Pat's Rubber Legs, small Woolly Bugger
- Green Weenie (#12–14) — regional confidence fly, inexplicably good
- Elk Hair Caddis, Parachute Adams (#14–18)
- Pheasant Tail / Prince nymph (#14–18)

> **Two hard DuPont rules:** no fishing within **300 feet of the top of any waterfall**, and **no night fishing** anywhere on the forest game lands. Both are actively enforced.

## West: Rosman & the French Broad headwaters

The **North, East, West, and Middle forks converge near Rosman to form the French Broad** — one of the oldest rivers in the world, and one of the few in North America that flows north. Rosman is the western hub; **Headwaters Outfitters** sits right at the confluence.

### East Fork French Broad — the DH workhorse

A roadside stream that has become one of WNC's most popular delayed-harvest waters, stocked along ~4.75 miles of East Fork Road. In the Oct–June C&R window this is the high-numbers day.

- Squirmy worm, egg patterns, Pat's Rubber Legs (#8–12)
- Green Weenie (#12–14)
- Zebra Midge, Pheasant Tail, Copper John (#16–20)
- Small Woolly Bugger / sculpin on cloudy days

### West Fork French Broad — big-brown sleeper

Produces some of the region's biggest browns each year. Trout-farm effluent in the headwaters makes it fecund and buggy, with unpredictable but heavy burrowing-mayfly hatches. Fewer fish, larger average size — a streamer-and-big-nymph stream.

- Sculpin / articulated streamers, olive & brown
- Large stonefly nymphs, Pat's Rubber Legs (#6–10)
- Big mayfly nymphs when the burrowers are active

### North Fork French Broad — pocket water

Runs through a rocky gorge with several waterfalls; fast runs and dark holes holding browns and rainbows averaging ~10". Wade with care.

- Heavy stonefly nymphs, streamers year-round
- Yellow Stimulator / Thunderhead in warm months
- High-floating attractor dries for the pocket water

## Southwest: the Cashiers / Highlands plateau

Higher, colder, and more remote — Highlands is one of the highest-elevation towns in the eastern US. This is where wild and native fish live alongside the plateau's marquee stocked runs. **Brookings Anglers** (Cashiers) is the anchor shop.

### Tuckasegee River ("the Tuck") — numbers king

The largest river in the Highlands-Cashiers area and the region's premier delayed-harvest float/wade. In the DH window, 50-fish days happen, and the **"Tuck Slam" (rainbow, brown, brook in one day)** is genuinely likely. This is the confidence water of the whole trip.

- Egg patterns, Squirmy worm, Pat's Rubber Legs (#8–12)
- Green Weenie, Copper John, Pheasant Tail (#12–18)
- Zebra Midge under an indicator in the deeper runs

### Chattooga River — wild & scenic

A federally designated Wild and Scenic River on the NC/SC/GA line; remote, pristine, and challenging, with wild brown, rainbow, and brook trout. Fish it for the setting and the wild fish, not the numbers.

- Yallarhammer (#10–14) — the traditional "skull crusher"
- Thunderhead, Adams Wulff (#12–16)
- Tellico Nymph, Prince nymph (#12–16)

### Cullasaja River (Highlands) — scenic roadside

Waterfalls (Dry Falls, Bridal Veil Falls on US-64), deep pools, and long glassy runs, with easy pull-offs between Turtle Pond Bridge and Gold Mine Bridge. Wild-ish and stocked trout; approachable.

- Parachute Adams, Elk Hair Caddis (#14–18)
- Pheasant Tail / Hare's Ear dropper
- Green Weenie in summer

### Whitewater River, Panthertown Valley & headwater brookies

Small, high, cold native brook-trout water — Panthertown ("the Yosemite of the East"), Whitewater, and the countless unnamed feeders. Short season, bluebird afternoons, dry-dropper on a short rod. Handle specks gently; they're the South's only native trout.

- Thunderhead, Yallarhammer, Yellow Stimulator (#12–16)
- Foam beetle / ant, Parachute Adams (#14–18)
- Small beadhead dropper (Pheasant Tail, #16–18)

## Seasonal windows

Southern Appalachian trout run on hatch timing and the DH calendar more than the four seasons. These windows are approximate — a warm February or a cold May shifts them a week or two — but they're the rhythm a local guide plans around. Confidence flies are the ones to tie on first if you have no other intel.

### Late winter → first hatches (mid-Feb – late March)

Cold, often high water; DH waters are in C&R and lightly pressured — the sleeper window for big stocked fish with the river to yourself. Wild-trout streams wake up with the year's first mayflies on the warmer afternoons. Fish midday, deep and slow.

- Confidence: **Zebra Midge / WD-40 (#18–22)**, **Blue Quill & Quill Gordon (#14–18)**, **Little Black Stonefly (#16)**, **Pheasant Tail (#16–18)**

### Peak spring dry-fly (April – mid-May)

The best wild-trout dry-fly fishing of the year and the Davidson's showpiece stretch. Hendricksons, March Browns, and Grannom caddis stack up; DH water is both C&R and freshly stocked — the fullest boxes-and-bent-rods window on the calendar.

- Confidence: **Hendrickson (#12–14)**, **March Brown (#12–14)**, **Elk Hair / Grannom Caddis (#14–16)**, **Parachute Adams (#14–18)**, **Pheasant Tail / Hare's Ear dropper**

### Sulphurs & the DH handoff (mid-May – first Saturday in June)

The Davidson's signature **Sulphur** hatch peaks — evening dry-fly fishing at its best. This is also the *last* of the DH C&R window before it flips to harvest, so hit the East Fork, Little River, and the Tuck hard before the first-Saturday-in-June changeover thins them out.

- Confidence: **Sulphur dun/emerger (#16–18)**, **Light Cahill (#14–16)**, **Yellow Sally (#14–16)**, **Green Weenie (#12–14)** on the stocked DH runs

### Early-summer terrestrials (mid-June – mid-July)

DH waters are now harvest regs and pressured — pivot toward freestone pocket water and the plateau. Terrestrials come online; Yellow Drakes and Isonychia show on the bigger water in the evenings. Mornings and evenings; midday slows.

- Confidence: **Yellow Stimulator (#12–16)**, **foam beetle / flying ant (#14–16)**, **Isonychia (#12–14)**, **Green Weenie (#12–14)**, **Tellico Nymph (#12–14)**

### High-summer low water & brookie prime (mid-July – early Sept)

Low, clear, warm freestones — fish the Davidson (stays cold below the hatchery) and go *up* for native brook trout, whose high, cold headwaters are at their best now. Dawn patrol and shaded plunge pools; skip the sun-baked lowland runs when water temps climb.

- Confidence: **Thunderhead (#12–16)**, **Yallarhammer (#10–14)**, **hopper-dropper** (foam hopper over a beadhead PT), **Parachute Adams / beetle (#14–18)**

### Fall reset: DH reopens (Sept – Oct)

**Oct 1 flips the DH waters back to C&R** with fresh, heavy stockings — the single biggest date on the calendar and prime numbers season. Brown trout stage toward their November spawn, so the streamer bite turns on. Fall BWOs on gray days. Arguably the best all-around window of the year.

- Confidence: **egg / Squirmy / Pat's Rubber Legs (#8–12)**, **sculpin & Woolly Bugger streamers**, **BWO dry/emerger (#18–20)**, **Green Weenie (#12–14)**

### Winter DH & midge season (Nov – mid-Feb)

The connoisseur's window: DH water is stocked, cold, and nearly empty of anglers. Browns are on/finishing the spawn (leave redds alone). Small-fly, deep-nymph, slow-swing fishing on the warmest hours of the day; midges do most of the work.

- Confidence: **Zebra Midge (black/red, #18–22)**, **midge larva / WD-40 (#20–22)**, **egg pattern (#14–16)**, **small streamer on the swing**

## Gear

A **9' 5wt with a floating line covers ~90% of this trip** — Davidson, the DH runs, and the French Broad forks. Two sensible additions:

- A **7'6"–8' 3wt or 4wt** for the small native brook-trout headwaters on the plateau and upper Pisgah — tight, rhododendron-choked casting where a 9-footer is a liability.
- A **6wt or a heavier leader/streamer rig** if you're chasing West Fork browns or throwing sculpins on the Tuck in higher water.

No stillwater-specific gear needed unless you plan to work DuPont's lakes (Cascade, Julia) hard — a spare intermediate line helps there. **Felt-sole restrictions don't apply in NC**, but bring studded rubber for the slick plateau bedrock regardless. A NC fishing license **plus the special trout privilege** is required; buy online before you go.

## Auxiliary info: shops, figures, towns & sights

### Fly shops and local figures

- **Davidson River Outfitters** (Pisgah Forest, at the mouth of the Davidson) — the anchor shop for the Brevard core, with a deep guide staff. Owner **Kevin Howell** is regional fly-fishing royalty: a Fly Fishing Masters champion and son of the late **Don Howell**, who with his brother **Dwight** ran the shop's forerunner and wrote the definitive regional guide, *Tying & Fishing Southern Appalachian Trout Flies* (1999, with Kevin contributing). If a name comes up here the way Harrop's does on the Henry's Fork, it's Howell.
- **Headwaters Outfitters** (Rosman, right at the French Broad confluence; second location in Brevard) — 30+ years running, the practical hub for the French Broad forks and the go-to for paddling/tubing shuttle logistics too.
- **Brookings Anglers** (Cashiers) — the plateau anchor, guiding the Tuckasegee, Chattooga, Whitewater, Panthertown, Cullasaja, and the small private/wild creeks. Best current intel for the Highlands/Cashiers leg.
- **Hookers Fly Shop** (Sylva, west of the plateau) — additional Tuckasegee and Smokies-side coverage if you push farther west.
- Worth knowing: the region has its own homegrown fly canon — the **Yallarhammer** (originally tied with feathers from the yellow-shafted flicker), the **Thunderhead** (Great Smoky Mountains tyer Fred Hall), the **Tellico Nymph**, the **Sheep Fly**, and the ubiquitous **Green Weenie**. A local guide will reach for these before your Western attractors.

### A short history of the region's flies

The Southern Appalachians tie their own tradition, older and more homespun than the Western tailwater canon. The mountain patterns began as wet flies and hair-wings meant for tumbling, tannic pocket water and unsophisticated wild fish — durability and floatation over exact imitation.

- **Yallarhammer** — the oldest and most storied of them, an old-timers' wet fly (later fished as a dry) originally tied with the barred wing and tail feathers of the yellow-shafted flicker, the "yellowhammer" of mountain speech. When the flicker came under the **Migratory Bird Treaty Act**, tyers moved to dyed-yellow substitute feathers, but the name and the reputation stuck — brookies hit it so hard it earned the nickname "skull crusher."
- **Tellico Nymph** — named for the Tellico River on the Tennessee side of the range, this yellow-bodied, peacock-and-brown nymph is the region's true breakout, the one homegrown pattern that went national and now sits in fly boxes far from Appalachia.
- **Thunderhead** — a dry fly from **Fred Hall**, a legendary Great Smoky Mountains tyer; a gray-brown-and-white, Adams-Wulff-ish attractor built to ride high through bubbly plunge pools. Named for Thunderhead Mountain in the Smokies, it remains the go-to high-country brook-trout dry.
- **Sheep Fly** — created by **Newland Saunders of Lenoir, NC** in the 1950s and popularized in the 1980s by **Don Howell** of Brevard, who said if he could fish one fly forever it would be this. It anchors the local canon alongside Don's own ties like "Don's Pet," and the Howell family's shop keeps it in the bins today.
- **Green Weenie** — the modern outlier: a simple chartreuse inchworm/caterpillar imitation adopted region-wide for reasons nobody fully explains. It just works, especially in summer, and no local box is without one.

Other names you'll still hear in the deep hollers — the **Jim Charley**, the **Smoky Mountain Forked Tail**, the **Tennessee Wulff** — round out a living tradition that Don Howell's 1999 book helped set down before it faded.

### Town notes

- **Brevard** (core base) — Transylvania County seat, home of the **Brevard Music Center** (summer institute since 1936) and famous for its **white squirrels** (there's a festival). Compact, walkable, brewery-dense downtown; the practical grocery/gear/lodging hub for the whole trip.
- **Pisgah Forest** (just north of Brevard) — the gateway up US-276 into Pisgah National Forest and the Davidson; **Sierra Nevada's East Coast brewery** is nearby in Mills River, and **Oskar Blues** is in Brevard proper.
- **Rosman** (west, French Broad hub) — a small former timber/rail town; nearby **PARI** (Pisgah Astronomical Research Institute) is a former NASA/NSA tracking station turned observatory, an unusual rainy-day stop.
- **Cashiers** (plateau) — a crossroads resort village at ~3,500 ft; upscale, seasonal, the practical stop for the Tuck/Chattooga leg.
- **Highlands** (plateau) — one of the highest incorporated towns in the eastern US (~4,100 ft), a genteel, cool-in-summer resort town with good restaurants and a waterfall-lined approach on US-64.

### Waterfalls & non-fishing sights

The "Land of Waterfalls" tag is earned — pair a bluebird afternoon with a drive:

- **Looking Glass Falls** & **Sliding Rock** (US-276, Pisgah) — roadside 60-ft falls and a natural waterslide, both right on the Davidson corridor.
- **DuPont's falls** (Hooker, Triple, High, Bridal Veil) — the *Hunger Games* / *Last of the Mohicans* set; short hikes, huge payoff.
- **Whitewater Falls** (near Cashiers) — one of the highest falls in the eastern US (~411 ft).
- **The Cullasaja Gorge** (US-64, Highlands) — Dry Falls (you can walk behind it) and Bridal Veil Falls, drive-through waterfalls on the way to the Cullasaja water.
