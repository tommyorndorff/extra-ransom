A one-piece split-cane **spinning** rod for winter fishing on the French Broad
around **Hap Simpson Park in Brevard**, late November through December. It's
milled full-length on the Morgan Hand Mill's extension bed. This is my first
spinning rod, so the page also covers the spinning basics a fly-rod builder
doesn't already know: power classes, the reel seat, guides, the reel, and lures.

> **The spec in one line:** 6'6", one piece, hex, **light power / moderate-fast
> action**, 4–6 lb line, lures **1/16–1/4 oz** (designed around 1/8 oz),
> sliding-ring cork grip, a 1000-size reel.

## The water: French Broad at Hap Simpson, Nov–Dec

- **Where:** Hap Simpson Park is a City of Brevard river access with a boat
  ramp and picnic tables, at about **mile 21.5** of the
  [French Broad Paddle Trail](https://frenchbroadpaddle.com/en/access-points).
  It's the take-out for the flat, calm ~10-mile float down from Island Ford.
  The Pisgah Forest access is about 5 miles downstream.
- **What it's like:** The mainstem here is slow, sandy, deep-bend water, not
  freestone pocket water. In winter, fish hold in the deep slow pools, on the
  outside bends, and around woody cover. Expect holdover stocked trout and
  resident browns, plus smallmouth, redbreast sunfish and rock bass, all
  sluggish in cold water.
- **Regulations (verify before fishing):** A third-party county guide lists
  the mainstem from the West/North Fork junction down to the US 276 bridge as
  **Hatchery Supported** trout water. That reach should include Hap Simpson,
  but I haven't confirmed it against the NCWRC rule text. Under Hatchery
  Supported rules: 7 trout/day, no minimum length, and **no bait or lure
  restrictions**. You need an NC fishing license **plus the trout privilege**
  on designated public mountain trout waters.
- **Best fishing nearby in this window:** The fresh fish are in the
  **Delayed Harvest** waters. These are the East Fork French Broad (NCWRC
  scheduled its fall 2026 stocking for **Nov 10** after warm water cancelled
  October) and DuPont's Little River. DH runs **catch-and-release,
  artificial lures with one single hook only**. Build the tackle box around
  single-hook lures and one kit covers both waters (see [Tackle](#tackle)).
- **Stocking caveat:** Bobby N. Setzer hatchery, right up the Davidson, closed
  in late 2025 for renovation, so NCWRC now stocks from Armstrong. Check the
  [NCWRC stocking schedule](https://www.ncwildlife.gov/delayed-harvest-schedule)
  instead of assuming past patterns.
- **Flows:** [USGS 03443000, French Broad at Blantyre](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/monitoring-location/03443000/)
  is the closest mainstem gage below Brevard.

The [Brevard / DuPont / Highlands dossier](/docs/fishing/2026-brevard-dupont-highlands-dossier/)
has the rest of the region: shops, the DH calendar, and Helene access notes.

## Power class: ultralight vs. light

Spinning rods are rated by **power** (how much force it takes to bend the
rod; roughly, the line and lure weights it handles) and **action** (where it
bends: fast = mostly the tip, moderate = into the middle). This is the
spinning equivalent of a fly rod's line weight.

| Class | Line | Lures | Good for | Here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ultralight (UL) | 2–4 lb | 1/32–1/8 oz | small creeks, small fish, short casts | Fits the East Fork and creeks. Underpowered for the mainstem's width, current, and the odd big brown or smallmouth. |
| **Light (L)** | **4–6 lb** | **1/16–1/4 oz** | small rivers, stocked trout, bass | **Pick this.** It casts across the mainstem pools, handles a 1/4 oz spoon or jerkbait, and is still fun on a 10" stocker. |
| Medium-light (ML) | 6–8 lb | 1/8–3/8 oz | bigger rivers, bass-first fishing | Overgunned for winter's small, slow lures. |

**Light wins** because this rod has to cover both the wide, slow mainstem and
the small DH runs, and winter lures are small (1/16–1/8 oz) but sometimes need
distance. A bamboo light rod still casts 1/16 oz well, because a cane rod's own
mass helps load it. Thomas & Thomas labeled their classic 5'6" spinner
"ultralight," and a builder who measured one rated it light-to-ultralight in
the hand. That's the feel I'm after here.

## Building it one piece

The Morgan Hand Mill's **extension bed** (a 36" bolt-on) handles one-piece rods
up to about **7'6"**, so a 6'6" rod fits with a foot to spare and **doesn't
need a splice**. Per the [manual notes](/docs/morgan-hand-mill-manual/):

- Each strip is cut on **both finishing anvils, switching once**. The tip-to-butt
  anvil changeover lands between bed stations **#7 and #8**.
- Mill-stop **hole B** for rough and finish butt; **hole A** for finish tip.
- Strip length = rod + 4" tip + 5" butt + 2" hold-down = **89"**.

What changes compared with the 2-piece class build:

- **Culms.** The class method cuts culms into 5–6' sections. A one-piece needs
  **~7'6"+ of clean culm** per strip. Take the strips from the lower 8' of a
  12' culm so the butt gets the dense power fibers. Expect 5–6 nodes per strip.
  Keep the 3×3 stagger, and keep nodes out of the top 6" (tiptop) and out of
  the reel-ring area.
- **Heat treating is the real constraint.** An 89" strip won't fit a kitchen
  oven. Options, best first:
  1. A **long tube oven**: an 8' steel or aluminum tube, heat gun, and a
     thermometer at each end. Flip the strips end for end halfway through,
     same as the forced-air note in the class sheet.
  2. **Flame the culm** before splitting. This is period-correct: Paul Young's
     1953 *Spin-Caster* was "medium flamed."
  3. **Splice.** Scarf-join a butt strip and a tip strip with a long (3–4")
     bevel. Stagger the six splices so no two land within ~6" of each other,
     and keep them out of the top 30" where the rod flexes most. This
     triples the glue-line risk, so use it only if 1 and 2 are out.
- **Handling.** A 7'+ strip whips around on the bench and in the binder. Support
  it on both ends while planing, and bind in one pass end to end.
- **Hanging to dry and storage.** You need a clear 8' vertical drop to hang the
  blank. A finished rod in a 6'10" tube rides diagonally in most cars.

>   the work.

## Handle and reel seat

The big difference from a fly rod is that **the reel sits partway up the
handle, not at the butt**, and it **hangs below the rod on a stem**. Your
casting hand wraps the seat with the reel stem between two fingers. The rear
grip runs back along your forearm, and your index finger reaches the spool to
feather the line. There's no line hand: the reel does all the line handling.

### Option A (recommended): full cork with sliding rings

The classic bamboo spinning handle (T&T, Paul Young, and the Montague-derived
6' on bamboorodmaking all use it). It's just a fly-rod cork grip with no seat
hardware. The reel foot sits on the cork and two metal rings slide over its
ends.

```goat
 tip                                                                  butt
 ---------+-+------------+------+-------------+------+------------+---+
  blank   | |    cork    | ring |  reel foot  | ring |    cork    |cap|
 ---------+ |            |      |             |      |            |   |
          +-+------------+------+----+---+----+------+------------+---+
       winding                       |   |  stem
        check                     .--+---+--.
                                 |   reel    |
                                  '---------'
          |<-------------------------- 12" cork ------------------------>|
          |<------- ~3.5" ---------->|<------------ ~8.5" --------------->|
       66" from tip                     reel-foot center            78" butt
```

- **12" Portuguese cork** (78" − 12" = cork starts at 66"). Shape it as one
  long cigar or a straight full-wells with a slight swell under the reel.
- **Rings:** 2 nickel-silver or aluminum sliding rings sized to the cork's
  diameter at the reel position, about 7/8–1". Lap the reel foot on the cork
  first; the rings should press it down snugly, not just touch.
- **Reel position:** about **8.5" from the butt end to the center of the reel
  foot**, then fine-tune by balance and forearm length. Hold it the way you'd
  fish it: the butt end should just reach the inside of your forearm.
- **Tradeoff:** the lightest, cleanest, and most period-correct option, but
  rings can creep when it's cold and wet. The fix is a strip of electrical
  tape under the foot or a slight taper in the cork so the rings jam.

### Option B: skeleton seat (modern, locks solid)

A graphite skeleton seat (Fuji and ALPS make spinning versions in 16–18 mm)
with split fore and rear grips. It's mounted on a built-up arbor over the hex
(masking tape or cork rings shaped round, epoxied).

```goat
 tip                                                                  butt
 ---------+-+----------+---------------------+--------------------+---+
          | |  fore    | hood   reel   hood  |     rear grip      |cap|
 ---------+ |  grip    |  [=== foot ===]  nut|     (cork/EVA)     |   |
          +-+----------+---------+--+--------+--------------------+---+
                                 |  | stem
                              .--+--+--.
                             |  reel    |
                              '--------'
           |<--  2.5" -->|<----- 3.5" ----->|<------- 6.5" ------->|
```

- Fixed hood on one end, threaded hood plus lock nut on the other. Either
  direction works. Pick whichever puts the threads where your fingers don't
  sit.
- **Tradeoff:** the reel never moves, but you'll have to make an arbor and it
  looks modern on cane.

### Fly vs. spin at a glance

| | Fly rod | Spinning rod |
|---|---|---|
| Reel position | at the butt, below the grip | 8–9" up from the butt, mid-handle |
| Reel orientation | hangs below | hangs below on a stem |
| What carries the cast | the line's weight | the lure's weight |
| Line control | line hand strips line | index finger on the spool, then turn the handle |
| Guides | small snakes, small stripper | fewer, **larger** single-foot guides; big stripper ring |
| Handed-ness | usually reel with the off hand | same: cast right, crank left (most reels swap sides) |

## Guides and tiptop

Line comes off a spinning spool in big coils, so the first guide (the
**stripper**) is large and sits high. The rest step down to choke the coils
into a straight line. Use light single-foot guides to keep weight off the
cane tip. A rule of thumb from the bamboorodmaking page is one guide per foot
of rod, plus one.

| Position (from tip) | Ring size | Notes |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0" | 6 mm ring, **6/64 tube** | across-corners at the tip ≈ 0.092", so 6/64 (0.094") fits |
| 5" | 6 mm | |
| 11" | 6 mm | |
| 18" | 7 mm | |
| 26" | 8 mm | |
| 35.5" | 10 mm | |
| 47" | **16 mm**, high frame | stripper, about 22" ahead of the reel foot |

**These are starting positions.** Tape the guides on, mount the reel, and run
the line through. Then do a **static load test**: tie the line off and bend the
rod to about 90°, then move guides until the line follows the blank's curve
without touching it. Then cast with tape before wrapping. Wrap with fine
thread, same as a fly rod; spinning guides don't need special wrapping.

## Reel

You want a **1000-size spinning reel** (Shimano 1000 / Daiwa LT 1000–2000)
that's light (≈5.5–6.5 oz) and holds ~100+ yd of 4–6 lb mono. A heavier reel will
make a 6'6" cane rod feel butt-heavy.

- **Modern pick: [Shimano Stradic FM 1000HG](https://www.tackledirect.com/shimano-st1000hgfm-stradic-fm-spinning-reel.html)**
  (SKU ST1000HGFM): ~185 g / 6.5 oz, 6.0:1 gear, 3 kg drag. It's the trout
  standard: smooth, durable, and easy to get serviced. *FM* is the current
  generation (the earlier one was *FL*), and *HG* means "high gear."
- **Co-pick: [Daiwa Ballistic MQ LT 1000](https://fieldandstream.com/outdoor-gear/fishing-gear/fishing-reels/best-trout-spinning-reels)**:
  6.2 oz on Field & Stream's 2026 test scale. It's the equal of the Stradic,
  so buy whichever is cheaper when you're ready.
- **Shop by spec, not by name:** 5.5–6.5 oz, 1000 size, aluminum spool, a
  drag that's smooth at low settings (you'll be running about 2 lb), and a
  handle that swaps sides. Published weights vary by source and model year,
  so confirm on the maker's spec sheet. If you can, mount the reel on a rod
  in a shop to feel the balance.
- **Period-correct on cane:** a vintage **Mitchell 308** ultralight (the
  **408** is its faster-geared sibling: blue, with a screw-in handle). Check
  which side the handle cranks on any one you buy, because the vintage models
  vary. Look for a serviced one, because drags and bail springs on
  50-year-old reels are the weak point.

Spinning basics for a fly angler:

- **Spool it right.** Line comes off the filler spool the same direction it
  goes onto the reel, or you'll fight twist all day. Fill to about 1/8" below
  the spool lip.
- **Set the drag** to about 1/3 of the line test. With 6 lb line, the drag
  should slip at a ~2 lb pull on a scale.
- **Casting:** pin the line on your index finger, open the bail, cast, and
  lift your finger to release. Touch the spool rim to feather or stop the
  lure. **Close the bail by hand**, not with the crank; that's what stops line
  twist and loops.
- **Spinners twist line.** Run a small ball-bearing swivel 18" ahead of any
  inline spinner.

## Tackle

Everything below is **single-hook or easy to convert**, so the same box works
on Hatchery Supported water (anything goes) and on the East Fork DH (one
single hook, no bait). For DH, swap trebles for single siwash hooks with split
rings and pliers, and take the second hook off a jerkbait.

**Line:** **6 lb mono** for the mainstem; 4 lb is optional on the small DH
runs. Mono is better than braid in December, because braid holds water and
ices in the guides on freezing mornings. A 3' 6 lb fluorocarbon leader is
optional in gin-clear water.

| Lure | Size | How to fish it in winter | DH-legal? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marabou or hair jig (black/olive, white) | 1/32–1/16 oz | Dead-drift or swim slowly along the bottom of deep pools. The top pick for cold water. | ✅ single hook |
| Trout Magnet–style micro plastic on a 1/64 oz head | 1/64 oz | Under a small float through the slow pools. Deadly on stockers. | ✅ |
| 2" curly-tail grub / small Ned rig | 1/16 oz head | Hop it slowly for winter smallmouth on the mainstem's deep bends. | ✅ |
| Inline spinner (Panther Martin, Mepps #0–1, Rooster Tail) | 1/16–1/8 oz | Retrieve slowly, just fast enough to turn the blade. Swap to a single hook for DH. | 🔧 convert |
| Spoon (Kastmaster, Phoebe) | 1/12–1/8 oz | Count it down, then flutter it back slowly. Good for long casts across the mainstem. | 🔧 convert |
| Small floating or suspending minnow (Rapala F3/F5, CD3) | ~1/8 oz | Twitch and pause. Big browns and smallmouth will hit it. | 🔧 one single hook |
| Nightcrawler on #8 hook + split shot | — | **Hatchery Supported water only** (bait banned on DH) | ❌ |

**Terminal kit:** small ball-bearing swivels, round split shot (BB–#4),
1/2" float, #8 and #10 single hooks, single siwash hooks for conversions, a
split-ring tool, forceps, a rubber-mesh net, and a small Plano box.

**How to fish it:** fish **11 a.m.–3 p.m.**, when the water is warmest. Fish
slow and deep, close to the bottom of the deepest slow pools and the outside
bends. Do several casts per spot before moving. Browns are spawning or just
finished, so **stay off redds**: the clean, light-colored gravel patches.

## Build checklist

1. Run the taper in Hexrod and compare it against Tyree's 6'. Adjust before
   cutting.
2. Map the 5" positions to extension-bed station numbers (TODO above).
3. Solve heat treating for 89" strips: tube oven, flamed culm, or splice.
4. Select 12' culms, take strips from the lower 8', and stagger nodes 3×3.
5. Rough → heat-treat → finish (both anvils, switching once) → dry bind →
   glue.
6. Shape the 12" cork; fit the reel foot and rings at about 8.5" from the butt.
7. Tape the guides and do the static load test, then a casting test. Then wrap
   and finish.
8. Buy the reel and line, then build the tackle box above.
9. Before the first trip, confirm the Hap Simpson reach's designation, the
   NCWRC stocking schedule, and USGS flows.
