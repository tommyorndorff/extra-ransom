Three solid, no-alcohol drinks, picked to cover different ground: creamy/tropical, bright/citrus, and a ginger-forward stand-in for a Moscow mule. No expertise assumed on my end either — these are straightforward builds, not bartending theory.

## Tools and stock worth having on hand

- Cocktail shaker (or any lidded jar/container that seals — a mason jar works)
- Jigger or a set of measuring shot glasses (1 oz / 2 oz)
- Citrus juicer or reamer — fresh juice matters a lot more than bottled here, especially for lime
- Muddler (the back of a wooden spoon works in a pinch) — for mint
- Fine strainer, if shaking anything with pulp or muddled mint
- Copper mugs, if leaning into the mule presentation — not required, just traditional
- Full-fat coconut milk (canned, not the carton beverage) and coconut water are different ingredients below — don't swap one for the other
- A bottle of good non-alcoholic ginger beer (strong ginger bite, well-carbonated) and a bottle of club soda or seltzer

## Coconut Colada

Creamy and tropical — the coconut mocktail to reach for over a piña colada. Full-fat canned coconut milk gives real body; coconut water alone comes out thin.

**Ingredients (1 drink):**
- 2 oz full-fat coconut milk (canned, shaken well first)
- 3 oz pineapple juice
- 0.5 oz fresh lime juice
- Splash of club soda or sparkling water, to top
- Ice
- Garnish: pineapple wedge or wheel, mint sprig

**Method:** Shake the coconut milk, pineapple juice, and lime juice hard with ice — the coconut milk wants the agitation to emulsify and get that piña-colada texture. Strain into a glass over fresh ice, top with a splash of soda, garnish.

*Variation:* swap the club soda for a splash of ginger beer if you want more spice against the coconut.

Source: [Be Well Table — Pineapple Coconut Mocktail](https://bewelltable.com/easy-pineapple-coconut-mocktail-recipe-non-alcoholic/).

## Blood Orange Citrus Spritz

Bright and a little tart — blood orange juice has more depth than regular orange juice, and the color is the best-looking drink of the three if blood oranges are in season (roughly winter into early spring in most US markets; regular orange juice works fine off-season, just less dramatic).

**Ingredients (1 drink):**
- 3 oz blood orange juice (fresh-squeezed if you can get blood oranges; bottled is fine otherwise)
- 0.5 oz fresh lime juice
- 0.5 oz agave syrup or simple syrup, to taste
- Sparkling water, to top
- Ice
- Garnish: orange wheel or twist

**Method:** Combine the juice, lime, and agave in a glass over ice, stir, then top with sparkling water. No shaking needed — this one's built, not mixed.

Source: [One Lovely Life — Blood Orange Mocktail](https://www.onelovelylife.com/blood-orange-mocktail/).

## The Not-a-Mule

A Moscow mule's whole appeal is ginger heat against lime and fizz — none of that depends on the vodka, so dropping it loses less than you'd think. This is the one worth serving in a copper mug if you own them; the cold-metal thing is real, not just presentation.

**Ingredients (1 drink):**
- 4 oz good non-alcoholic ginger beer (strong ginger, well-carbonated — the quality of the ginger beer is the whole drink here)
- 0.75–1 oz fresh lime juice
- Splash of club soda, optional, for extra fizz if the ginger beer alone isn't lively enough
- Ice
- Garnish: lime wheel, mint sprig

**Method:** Fill a copper mug (or any glass) most of the way with ice. Add lime juice, top with ginger beer, stir gently — stir, don't shake, or the ginger beer goes flat. Top with a splash of club soda if you want more sparkle. Garnish and serve immediately.

Source: [Yummy Mummy Kitchen — Virgin Moscow Mule](https://www.yummymummykitchen.com/2019/12/moscow-mule-mocktail-recipe.html), [Whisk Affair — Virgin Moscow Mule](https://www.whiskaffair.com/virgin-moscow-mule-recipe/).
