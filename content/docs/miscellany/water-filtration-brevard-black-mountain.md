Whole-house and kitchen-sink filters for two homes (two people each), one on city water in Brevard and one in Black Mountain. The goal is a practical balance of price and value, so this page starts from what the utilities actually report and only buys what that data justifies.

> **Status (2026-11-23):** research only, nothing bought or installed. Prices are retail snapshots from late 2026 and move often, so re-check before ordering. Black Mountain has no PFAS data and no hardness figure for its wells, so test the tap there before spending much (see [Open questions](#open-questions)).

## What's in the water

From each town's 2025 Consumer Confidence Report (CCR), the annual water quality report every utility must publish.

| | **Brevard** | **Black Mountain** |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Cathey's Creek (surface water) | About 49% Asheville surface water, about 51% town wells (10 wells, one entry point) |
| Disinfectant | Chlorine, avg 1.34 ppm (0.8–1.7) | Chlorine, avg 1.36 ppm (0.7–1.9); Asheville portion avg 1.21 |
| Hardness | ~4 ppm (third-party site, not in the CCR) | Asheville portion 5.5 ppm; wells not reported |
| pH | Not in the CCR | 7.4 (wells), 7.6 (Asheville) |
| TTHM (limit 80 ppb) | Avg 51, range <1–81 | Avg 27, range 0–43 |
| HAA5 (limit 60 ppb) | Avg 50, range 27–75 | Avg 36 |
| Lead | None detected | At or under 14 ppb at the highest tap (action level 15) |
| Sodium | Not in the CCR | 150 ppm (wells, Dec 2023 sample) |
| PFAS | None detected in the 2023 EPA screening round (third-party site) | Not found |
| Violations | None in 2025 | Chlorine and E. coli monitoring lapses after Hurricane Helene, back in compliance early 2025 |

What that means:

- **Both towns use plain chlorine, not chloramine.** Ordinary carbon removes chlorine, so a basic carbon filter does well in both.
- **Both waters are very soft.** A softener would do nothing useful; skip it.
- **Brevard's disinfection byproducts run high.** The annual averages comply, but the HAA5 peak of 75 ppb is above the 60 ppb limit and TTHM peaked at 81. Carbon filtration reduces both.
- **Black Mountain's wells are the unknown.** The 150 ppm sodium reading is unusually high (it could be an outlier), and iron, manganese and hardness for the well side are not in the report.

## Recommended setup

| | Brevard | Black Mountain |
|---|---|---|
| Whole house | Sediment + carbon (DIY Big Blue) | Sediment + carbon (DIY Big Blue) |
| Kitchen sink | Under-sink carbon block (Aquasana AQ-5200) | Under-sink reverse osmosis (iSpring RCC7AK) |
| Rough total | $400–$550 | $500–$700 |

Why the split: Brevard's problem is chlorine and byproducts, which carbon handles. Black Mountain's is the unknown well water and the sodium, and reverse osmosis (RO) is the only affordable under-sink option that removes sodium and gives margin on anything the missing PFAS data might hide.

## Whole house

For two people the flow demand is low, so a 20″ Big Blue two-stage setup is enough and is the best value. A packaged system is the low-effort alternative.

| Option | Price (late 2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Big Blue 20″ housing, 1″ ports (Home Depot) | ~$72 each; two needed | 1st housing sediment, 2nd carbon |
| Pentair 20″ 5-micron sediment system (Home Depot) | ~$159 | Single-stage system, add a second housing for carbon |
| APEC 3-stage 20″ Big Blue housing set, model FH-3BB-20 | Check the product page | Three housings; the third is not needed here |
| Sediment + carbon cartridge sets, 4.5″ × 20″ | ~$79–$230 | Cheapest is a 5-micron sediment, charcoal and carbon set; APEC's sediment, carbon and iron set is ~$230 |
| SpringWell CF1 (packaged system) | ~$1,105–$1,160 (MSRP $1,365) | Install is extra; replacement pre-filters ~$20 each every 6–9 months |

**Estimate for the DIY route:** two housings (~$144), a starter cartridge set (~$80–$160), plus shutoff valves, a pressure gauge and fittings (~$50–$100), so roughly **$300–$400 per house**, plus cartridges each 6–12 months. The CF1 costs about three times as much for less fuss; I'd only buy it if I didn't want to touch plumbing.

## Kitchen sink

| Option | Price (late 2026) | Pick for |
|---|---|---|
| Aquasana AQ-5200 2-stage carbon | ~$106–$125 (Walmart from $106, Aquasana direct $125) | Brevard |
| iSpring RCC7AK 6-stage RO with remineralization | ~$202–$341 depending on retailer and faucet (Amazon deal $202, Walmart black faucet $250, Lowe's nickel faucet $341) | Black Mountain |

Notes:

- The AQ-5200 is the cheapest option that addresses lead and chlorine byproducts. Check its current certification sheet for the specific claims you care about (lead, VOC, PFAS) before buying, since cartridge certifications change.
- The RCC7AK is NSF certified and adds a remineralizing stage, which helps because RO water from already-soft water is very low in minerals. Expect a drain line and a storage tank under the sink.

## Open questions

- **PFAS in Black Mountain.** Not found in the Town's report. Ask the town (828-419-9300) for its well and Asheville PFAS results, or test the tap with a lab.
- **Black Mountain well hardness, iron and manganese.** If iron or manganese is present, the sediment-only stage is not enough and an iron cartridge (APEC sells a sediment, carbon and iron set) is worth adding.
- **The 150 ppm sodium.** One sample from Dec 2023; confirm with the next report or a tap test before treating it as normal.
- **Brevard hardness and pH.** Not in the CCR; the ~4 ppm hardness figure is from a third-party aggregator.
- **Installation details.** Whole-house placement (before the water heater, shutoff and bypass locations) has not been planned for either house.
