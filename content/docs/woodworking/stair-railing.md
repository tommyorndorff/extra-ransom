An **in-progress** project: a white oak stair railing built from 4/4 stock, with a flat top rail and plywood art panels hung on hidden cleats between the rails. The drawing set is a 2-page PDF (side elevation + cleat/panel detail); this page is its home while the build comes together.

<a href="/static/woodworking/stair_railing_oak_4-4_cleat_panels.pdf">Drawing set (PDF, 2 pages)</a>

## Design at a glance

- **Material:** white oak, S3S, 4/4 (posts 8/4); panels are 3/4" plywood
- **Slope:** 35.3° (measured), assuming a 7-1/16″ riser and 10″ tread — verify on the actual stair
- **Run:** 98″ (8′-2″) along the slope; 80″ clear between posts, 87″ overall including posts
- **Top rail:** 1-5/8″ × 3″ (two 4/4 boards face-glued), laid flat; top of rail 36″ above the nosing line
- **Posts:** 4×4 (8/4 stock)
- **Infill:** 5 panels, 12″ wide, with gaps of 3-1/3″ (all under 4″)
- **Panel mounting:** single-4/4 oak cleats (13/16″) hidden behind the panels, set 3/4″ in from the rail's outside edge so the panel face is flush with the rail edge; the panel butts the rail underside, outside the cleat, and is screwed into the cleat (three 1-5/8″ screws per cleat)
- **Cleat fastening:** glued to the rail, plus a 2″ stainless screw every 12″ driven up through the cleat into the rail; the bottom rail is the mirror image (cleat on top)
- **Panel opening:** 29-1/64″ vertical between the rails

## Notes and open items

- Test the real art openings against a 4″ sphere — the drawing only checks the 3-1/3″ gaps.
- Use stainless or coated fasteners with white oak (tannin staining).
- Panel art in the drawing is a placeholder.
- Dimensions come from an assumed riser/tread; re-measure before cutting.
