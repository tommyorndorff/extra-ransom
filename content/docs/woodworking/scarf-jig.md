---
title: "Adjustable Scarf Jig (Proposed)"
weight: 27
---

A **proposed** shop jig for cutting long, accurate scarf bevels in strip
stock: the 4½" splice on [Rod 002](/docs/rodmaking/builds/002-french-broad-spinning-rod/)'s
bamboo sections, and also scarfed trim, bow and paddle laminations, and
anything else that needs a feathered lap joint. It's a permanent tool, not
a one-off. One knob sets any ratio from **1:8 to 1:40**, and the setting
doesn't depend on the stock's thickness.

> **How it works.** Two hard-maple rails form a flat reference plane. A
> sanding beam rides on them and can't cut below that plane. Between the
> rails is a **tilting bed hinged so that its top surface pivots on the
> rail plane**. Clamp the stock with its end on the hinge line and drop the
> far end of the bed. The stock then rises above the rails by exactly the
> wedge you want removed. Sand until the beam rides the rails, and the
> bevel feathers out precisely at the end of the stock. **The only setting
> is the ratio:** the bed's drop at the 12″ mark is d = 12″ ÷ ratio, for
> any thickness of stock.

## Holding the work

**A C-clamp won't work on the bed itself.** The bed sits recessed in a 3″
channel between 3″-tall rails, with the base underneath, so a clamp's
lower jaw has nowhere to go. Anything sticking up above the rail plane
inside the scarf would also be hit by the beam. The hold comes in three
layers instead:

1. **Double-sided tape under the scarf zone (the primary hold).** Use thin
   cloth carpet tape or turner's tape, along the whole scarf and a few
   inches past it. It's the only thing that can hold the **feather**, the
   last thin inch that no clamp can reach. On bamboo, press the section
   down hard for a minute. Release it afterward with a putty knife and a
   little naphtha.
2. **The hold-down bar** goes across the stock's top flat, knobbed into the
   nearest insert pair past the beam's travel. It resists the pull of each
   stroke. For a scarf of length L, use the first pair beyond **L + 3″**,
   and use a short beam (6″ long) on short scarfs so it stops short of the
   bar. For the 4½″ bamboo splice, that's the 9″ pair. Pad the bar so it
   doesn't mark the enamel side.
3. **The outboard end gets the C-clamp.** A rod section is 39″ long and
   leaves the jig sloping gently downhill (2.5° at 1:23). Support it on a
   block or roller stand at that height, and C-clamp it there. That's where
   a C-clamp is useful: it keeps the long lever arm from rocking the stock
   on the bed.

Sand with light strokes **toward the feather**. That pulls the stock
toward the hinge, against the tape and the bar, and keeps bamboo fibers
from lifting at the thin end.

## Settings

**Drop d** is measured at the 12″ setting mark, from a straightedge laid
across the rails down to the bed's top. Use a depth gauge or the
depth rod of your calipers.

| Ratio | d at 12″ mark | Bed angle | Max stock thickness (L ≤ 15″) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1:8 | 1.500″ | 7.1° | 1.88″ |
| 1:10 | 1.200″ | 5.7° | 1.50″ |
| 1:12 | 1.000″ | 4.8° | 1.25″ |
| 1:16 | 0.750″ | 3.6° | 0.94″ |
| 1:20 | 0.600″ | 2.9° | 0.75″ |
| **1:23.3 (Rod 002 splice)** | **0.515″** | 2.5° | 0.64″ |
| 1:24 | 0.500″ | 2.4° | 0.62″ |
| 1:30 | 0.400″ | 1.9° | 0.50″ |

- **Any other ratio:** d = 12 ÷ r. For a scarf of length L on stock of
  thickness t, r = L ÷ t.
- **Capacity:** stock up to 2-3/4″ wide. Scarf length L up to 15″, so the
  maximum thickness is 15 ÷ r.
- **Ratio range:** at 1:8 the bed's far end still clears the base by 0.27″,
  which sets the steep limit (~1:7.5). Shallow ratios are only limited by
  how small a d you can measure.

## 3D-printed parts

A 3D printer makes several of the fiddly parts easier, and repeatable.
Print them in PETG, which is tougher than PLA and doesn't creep under a
clamp, at 4+ perimeters and 40% infill.

| Part | Why print it |
|---|---|
| **Ratio setting gauges** | A stepped block whose steps are the drop **d** for 1:8, 1:12, 1:16, 1:20, 1:23.3 and 1:24. Set it on the bed at the 12″ line and raise the bed until the step for your ratio touches a straightedge across the rails. No calipers needed, and the setting repeats. Print it on its side so the steps are accurate, and check them with calipers once. |
| **Hold-down clamps** | Low-profile cam clamps or bars with a TPU pad, sized to sit under the beam's path where they can. For narrow stock, a bar with a shallow relief so it presses on the center of the top flat. |
| **Fence strip** | A thin printed fence with tabs that key into the insert holes, so it registers square to the hinge line every time instead of being taped on. |
| **End-alignment gauge** | A small block that rests on both rails and drops a lip exactly to the hinge line. Push the stock's end against the lip, then lift the gauge away. It's faster and more accurate than sighting with a square. |
| **Beam handles** | A comfortable handle (and a short-beam version) bolted to the MDF or aluminum beam. |

The rails, bed and base should stay **wood**: their flatness and stiffness
are the whole tool. Printed parts only hold, locate and set.

## Build

1. **Mill the rails and bed** from the same quartersawn board. Plane them
   to final thickness together so they move together.
2. **Glue and screw the rails** to the base's long edges, with their outside
   faces flush. Put the screws in from underneath so nothing is near the
   rail tops. Add the feet.
3. **Drill the hinge together.** Shim the bed so its top is flush with the
   rail tops, and clamp everything. Then drill **one ¼″ hole straight
   through rail, bed, and rail** at X = 5″, 3/8″ below the top. That's
   2-5/8″ above the base's top. Drilling it in one pass is what keeps the
   bed's top surface pivoting on the rail plane.
   - **The pin goes through the bed.** The hole is centered in the bed's ¾″
     thickness, with ¼″ of wood above and below the pin. Leave 3/8″ of bed
     behind the hole and round that nose to a 3/8″ radius about it. Because
     the nose is centered on the pin, its highest point stays at the rail
     plane at every tilt, so it never sticks up into the beam's path.
   - **Fits:** the pin is a press fit in the rails. Open the **bed's** hole
     just enough for the bed to turn without play, with a ¼″ reamer or 320
     grit wrapped on a dowel. A sloppy 17/64″ hole would let the feather
     point wander.
4. **Flatten the reference plane.** With the bed shimmed flush and clamped,
   sand the **rail tops and the bed top together** on a flat surface, such as
   PSA paper on a jointer bed, a cast-iron wing, or glass. They must be one
   continuous plane. Check with a straightedge: the bed flush to the rails
   within 0.002″ is d = 0.
5. **Lift screw:** install the T-nut under the base at X = 17″. Epoxy the
   brass pad under the bed at that spot, and thread in the thumb screw and
   jam nut.
6. **Marks:** scribe the **hinge line** (X = 5″) and the **12″ setting line**
   (X = 17″) across both rail tops, and ink them.
7. **Hold-down:** install the two inserts in the bed, and make the bar and
   the fence strip.
8. **Sanding beam:** flatten the MDF, stick on the PSA paper, and add a
   handle. Wax the rail tops so the beam glides.

## Using it

1. **Zero:** with d = 0, a straightedge should lie flat across rails and bed.
2. **Set the ratio:** turn the lift screw until d at the 12″ line matches
   the settings table, then lock the jam nut.
3. **Load the stock:** lay it on the bed against the fence, with its **end
   exactly on the hinge line**. Sight down the scribed line with a square,
   then hold it as described in [Holding the work](#holding-the-work).
4. **Sand:** take long, light strokes along the jig, starting with 80 grit.
   Keep the beam flat on both rails. You're done when the beam rides the
   rails along the whole scarf and the end has feathered to nothing. Finish
   with 120 and then 220.
5. **The mating piece** gets the same setting and the same procedure. Joined
   face to face, the two bevels restore the full section.

> **Accuracy.** The pin sits 3/8″ below the bed's top surface, so the pivot
> line moves very slightly as the bed tilts. At 1:8 the feather point shifts
> 0.05″ along the stock and drops 0.003″; at 1:23 it's about a third of
> that. That's well inside the material you trim off the feathered tip
> anyway.

## Rod 002 splice, worked example

- **Joint:** 4½″ scarf at the 39″ joint, where the hex measures 0.193″
  across the flats.
- **Ratio:** r = 4.5 ÷ 0.193 = **1:23.3**, so d = 12 ÷ 23.3 = **0.515″**.
- **Tip section:** lay it **flat-side down** on the bed with its end on the
  hinge line. A hex rests stably on a flat, so the bevel runs across the
  flats. Tape it and hold it down at the far end, then sand with light
  strokes. Bamboo fibers want to lift at the feather: finish with 220 and
  keep strokes going **toward the feather**.
- **Butt section:** same setting, same procedure.
- **Mate them:** rotate one section 180° about its axis so the bevels face
  each other. Line up the guide flats, whip the feather ends, and tape the
  joint, as described on the Rod 002 page.

## Variations

- **Router sled for thick stock.** A 6″ × 12″ sled spanning the rails can
  carry a trim router with a flat-bottom bit set flush with the sled's
  underside. Run it back and forth along the rails. It's faster than sanding
  on 1″+ hardwood. Take conventional (not climb) passes, and use a full hold-down.
- **Longer scarfs:** lengthen the rails and bed together. Keep the 12″
  setting mark, and capacity grows with the bed.
- **Metric:** scribe a second mark 300 mm from the hinge; d = 300 mm ÷ r there.
