A **proposed** build: a small plywood cart on locking casters for a DeWalt
DW745 10" jobsite table saw. It's deliberately compact. The footprint is
just over the saw's table, it comes out of **one sheet of plywood**, and
it holds the saw's accessories underneath. The saw comes off with four
knobs when it needs to go to a jobsite.

- **Footprint:** 28" W × 24" D. The DW745's table is about 630 × 570 mm
  (24.8" × 22.4"), so the cart is just over 1" bigger on each side.
- **Saw table height:** **34"** from the floor, a standard bench height, so
  a workbench can serve as outfeed. Some shops prefer ~35.5" (900 mm); pick
  yours and plug it into the formula below.
- **Casters:** 4 of my **step-stop locking casters**, one at each corner.
- **Material:** ¾" plywood (birch or good-one-side), glue and screws.

## Set the height first

Two measurements decide the carcass height. Take them before cutting
anything:

1. **s**: from the bottom of the saw's base (where it'll sit on the cart)
   to the top of the saw table.
2. **c**: from the caster's mounting plate to the floor.

**Carcass height H = 34" − s − c.** That's the outside height of the box,
top and bottom panels included.

| Example (yours will differ) | s | c | **H** | Interior (H − 1½") |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3" casters | 13.5" | 3.5" | **17"** | 15.5" |
| 4" casters | 13.5" | 4.5" | **16"** | 14.5" |
| 5" casters | 13.5" | 5.5" | **15"** | 13.5" |

The s = 13.5" here is only a placeholder; I don't have a published
base-to-table height for the DW745. Measure your saw.

## Cut list (one 4×8 sheet of ¾" plywood)

Cut sizes for the example **H = 16"** (interior 14.5"). If your H is
different, change the 14.5" dimensions to match your interior height.

| Part | Qty | Size | Notes |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Top | 1 | 28" × 24" | Saw mounts here |
| Bottom | 1 | 28" × 24" | Casters mount here |
| Sides | 2 | 14.5" × 24" | Captured between top and bottom |
| Back | 1 | 14.5" × 26.5" | Between the sides; squares and stiffens the box |
| Shelf | 1 | 26.5" × 22.75" | Set 1" back from the front edge, at mid-height |

That's about 22 ft² of the sheet's 32 ft². The offcut is enough for push
sticks and a cord cleat.

## Hardware

| Qty | Item | Use |
|---:|---|---|
| 4 | Step-stop locking casters (on hand) | one per corner, plates flush to the corners |
| 16 | 5/16" carriage bolts, 1½", with washers and nylock nuts | through-bolt the caster plates; match your plate holes |
| 4 | ¼"-20 T-nuts | in the top, under the saw's base mounting holes |
| 4 | ¼"-20 star knobs (2" studs) + fender washers | hold the saw down; removable without tools |
| ~40 | 1⅝" coarse wood screws | carcass |
| — | Wood glue, edge banding (optional) | |
| 2 | Cord cleats or hooks (offcut) | on one side, for the cord |

## Build

1. **Box:** glue and screw the sides to the bottom, then add the back
   between the sides. Check that it's square, then add the top. Fit the
   shelf on cleats or dados at mid-height.
2. **Casters:** bolt one under each corner of the bottom, plates as close to
   the corners as they'll go. A wider wheelbase means a less tippy cart.
3. **Saw mount:** set the saw centered on the top, with the blade parallel to
   the cart's sides, and mark its base mounting holes through from above.
   Drill and install the ¼"-20 T-nuts from underneath, then hold the saw down
   with the four knobs.
4. **Finish:** ease the edges and give it a coat of wipe-on poly or paste
   wax so sawdust doesn't stick.

## Using it safely

- **Lock all four casters before you turn the saw on.** Step-stop casters
  that lock both the wheel and the swivel are what you want. A cart that
  creeps during a rip is a kickback risk.
- **Keep heavy items on the bottom shelf.** The saw makes the cart
  top-heavy, and weight down low keeps it planted.
- **Support long stock.** On a footprint this small, don't rip sheet goods or
  long boards without infeed and outfeed support. At 34" a workbench or
  roller stand works.
- **Keep the rear dust port clear**, and run a shop-vac hose off the back
  instead of through the cart.

## Buy instead?

DeWalt makes rolling stands for this saw, the **DE7400** wheeled stand and
the **DW7451** compact stand. They fold, they're quick, and they cost more
than this cart. They're the better choice if the saw travels a lot. This
cart wins in the shop, with storage, a solid base, and casters you already
own.
