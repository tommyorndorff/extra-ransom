A **proposed** build: a small plywood cart on locking casters for a DeWalt
DW745 10" jobsite table saw, designed so the saw can live in the garage
with **as little dust as possible**. A jobsite saw's body is an open frame,
with holes on every side and an open bottom. A box under it would only
catch what falls straight down, so this cart **encloses the saw body on all
four sides**:
- a **½" plywood skirt** stands on the cart top and rises to a brush seal
  under the table;
- everything the saw throws out its sides lands inside the enclosure;
- a steep **V-hopper** below feeds a full-width **slotted 4" manifold** to
  my Rockler 650 CFM collector.

The footprint is still just over the saw's table. The saw still lifts out
with four knobs.

- **Footprint:** 28" W × 24" D. The DW745's table is about 630 × 570 mm
  (24.8" × 22.4", per a retailer listing).
- **Saw table height:** **34"** from the floor, a standard bench height, so
  a workbench can serve as outfeed. Plug your preferred height into the
  formula below.
- **Enclosure:** the skirt's outside is 24" × 21". That's inside the table's
  footprint, so its top edge sits under the table, where a nylon brush
  strip seals it. Inside, it's 23" × 20" for the saw body.
- **Below:** two ~50° ramps form a V that meets over a slotted 4" PVC
  manifold. The manifold exits the left side, so the cart can sit against a
  wall.
- **Storage:** a small drawer under the front ramp. The saw's onboard
  fence and miter-gauge storage ends up inside the skirt, so hang those on
  hooks on the cart's right side.
- **Casters:** 4 of my **step-stop locking casters**, plus ~25 lb of
  ballast sealed in under the back ramp.

**What's enclosed.** The skirt, cart top, hopper and manifold make one
sealed volume of about **4.4 ft³** around and under the saw. The saw's
side vents, bevel slot, open bottom and rear dust port all empty into it.
The rear port can simply be turned to point down. Air gets in only from
above the table:
- the blade slot and the throat-plate gap;
- a little leakage through the brush seals;
- the brushed openings in the front skirt.

That's the point: **every path for air runs into the enclosure, not out of
it.** At 350–450 CFM the enclosure's air is replaced 80–100 times a minute.

**Openings in the front skirt.** The DW745's controls are on the front, so
the front panel gets two brushed openings:
- **Handwheel arc slot.** The blade-height handwheel moves along an arc as
  the blade bevels. Template the slot by taping cardboard over the front of
  the saw, running the bevel from 0° to 45°, and tracing the shaft's path.
  Cut the slot about ½" wider than the shaft, then close it with two strips
  of brush seal facing each other. Take the handwheel off to fit the panel,
  then put it back on outside.
- **Switch window.** A cutout around the paddle switch, edged with brush or
  foam, so it stays reachable. A clear acrylic window over the bevel scale
  is optional.

**Hopper and manifold.** Two ¼" hardboard ramps run from the edges of the
cart-top opening down to the crown of a **4" PVC sewer & drain pipe** lying
across the bottom at mid-depth. They're about **50°**, well past sawdust's
35–45° angle of repose.
- **Slot:** about 22" long, cut along the pipe's top between the two ramp
  feet. Taper it from ½" wide at the exit end to ¾" at the closed end so
  the pull is even.
- **Ends:** the pipe's far end butts the right side panel, which caps it.
  The other end passes through the left side and sticks out ~1½" for the
  collector hose.
- **Offcut guard:** the slot is too narrow for an offcut to reach the
  single-stage collector's impeller.

**The numbers.**
- **Airflow:** the collector is rated at 650 CFM free air. With a short
  hose and this box, expect **350–450 CFM**.
- **Duct and slot speed:** that's **4,000–5,200 ft/min** in the 4" duct and
  about 3,700–4,700 ft/min through the slot, both above the ~3,500 ft/min
  that keeps chips moving.
- **Hose:** keep the hose to the collector short (≤ 10 ft) and
  smooth-walled.

## Above the table: the part a cabinet can't catch

For **truly minimal** garage dust, plan on four more things. The enclosure
handles everything below the table, but the top of the blade still throws
fine dust toward you.

1. **Over-blade collection.** A blade guard with a dust port, or an
   overarm guard, run on a 2½" line to a **4 × 4 × 2½" wye** on the collector
   hose. Check that one fits the DW745 before buying; the stock guard has no
   port.
2. **A fine filter on the collector.** Single-stage bag collectors often pass
   the finest dust straight back into the room. Check your bag's micron
   rating, and upgrade to a 1-micron bag or canister if it's coarser.
3. **An ambient air filter** running during and after cutting.
4. **A respirator** for long sessions. Collection reduces dust; it doesn't
   eliminate the fine fraction.

## Measure first

Three measurements set the build. Take them all before cutting:

1. **Body clearance:** the saw body's footprint below the table must fit
   inside **23" × 20"** (the skirt's inside) **through full blade-height
   and 0–45° bevel travel**. If it doesn't, enlarge the skirt. Its outside
   still has to stay inside the table's footprint.
2. **s:** from the saw's feet to the top of the table.
3. **g:** from the saw's feet to the lowest point under the table's
   perimeter, which is usually the fence rails. The skirt's top goes ¼"
   below that, and the brush closes the gap.

Then:
- **Carcass height H = 34" − s − c − 1½"**, where c is the caster height
  (mounting plate to floor) and 1½" is the saw's support rails.
- **Skirt height = g + 1½" − ¼"**, measured from the cart top.

| Example (yours will differ) | s | c | **H** | Interior | Ramp angle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3" casters | 12" | 3.5" | **17"** | 15.5" | ~53° |
| 4" casters | 12" | 4.5" | **16"** | 14.5" | ~50° |
| 5" casters | 12" | 5.5" | **15"** | 13.5" | ~47° |

The s = 12" and the 11" skirt in the example are placeholders; I don't have
published DW745 figures. Ramp angle = atan((interior − 4.2") ÷ 8.6"). The
4.2" is the pipe crown; 8.6" is each ramp's run.

## Build

The order matters: several parts get sealed in early.

1. **Measure** (see above) and adjust the drawings to your saw.
2. **Bottom panel:** bolt the caster plates on with the heads inside.
   Counterbore the front pair.
3. **Manifold:** cut the tapered slot along the pipe, then bore the 4¼″
   hole in the left side.
4. **Box:** glue and screw the sides to the bottom. Slide the pipe in, at
   mid-depth with its slot facing up, and fit the back. Caulk the pipe to the
   bottom and to the right side, which caps its end.
5. **Under the ramps:** set the ballast behind the pipe. Fit the drawer-bay
   back 6″ behind the front panel's position, then the ramp cleats.
6. **Ramps:** glue and screw them onto the cleats so their feet land on the
   slot's two edges. Caulk every edge.
7. **Front panel, then the top:** caulk the wall tops and the ramp heads, then
   glue and screw on the top with its 21″ × 18″ opening.
8. **Support rails:** place the saw upside down on cardboard and mark its
   feet. Set the two rails on the ledges under those marks and fit the
   T-nuts. Screw the rails down.
9. **Skirt:** cut the four panels and fit the back and sides with cleats to
   the cart top. Set the saw on its rails and knob it down. **Template the
   front panel** (arc slot and switch window) against the actual saw. Then
   remove the handwheel, fit the front panel, and refit the handwheel
   outside it. Caulk the skirt to the top inside.
10. **Brush seal:** run brush strip along the skirt's top edges so the
    bristles just press against the table's underside and fence rails, and
    line the arc slot and switch window. Turn the saw's rear port to point
    down, or remove its adapter.
11. **Test:** run the collector. Check that incense held at the blade throat
    and around the brush seals is pulled **in**, then run the blade through
    full bevel and height and confirm nothing rubs.
12. **Drawer and hooks:** build the drawer slideless and wax it, then hang
    the fence and miter gauge on the right side.

## Using it safely

- **Never run the saw with the collector off.** The DW745's motor cools
  itself with air, and inside a closed skirt it would recirculate its own
  heat. Start the collector first, and run it 10–15 seconds after the saw
  stops.
- **Lock all four casters before you turn the saw on.** Point the swivels
  outward as you lock them, which widens the stance. Step-stop casters
  that lock both the wheel and the swivel are what you want.
- **Mind the tipping point.** Loaded and ballasted, the cart weighs about
  125 lb. A push of roughly **38 lb front-to-back** or **46 lb sideways**
  at table height would tip it. Support long stock with a workbench or
  roller stand.
- **Offcuts and blade changes:** offcuts that fall through the throat land
  on the hopper. Unplug the saw, lift it off (four knobs), and clear them.
  Blade changes happen from above, through the throat plate, as usual. Never
  reach in through the blade opening.
- **Check your collector's specs.** Rockler lists its 650 CFM Dust Right
  wall-mount (TADC-34WN) as ¾ hp. Measure your inlet and size the manifold
  stub to match.

## Buy instead?

DeWalt's **DW7451** compact stand is listed for the DW745. It folds and it's
quick, but it has no dust control at all. It's the better choice if the saw
travels a lot. This cart is built for one job: a jobsite saw in a garage
with the least dust that a saw like this can practically manage.
