A winter garage changes three things: **there is no sunlight, it is cold, and there is moisture around electricity.** All three have fixes.

## Light

Garage windows will not do it. Plants need a dedicated light.

- **Seedlings:** roughly 100–200 µmol/m²/s (PPFD) at the canopy, 14–16 h/day.
- **Leafy greens and herbs in the tower:** roughly 200–400 µmol/m²/s, 14–16 h/day.
- **Fruiting crops** (tomatoes, peppers, strawberries): considerably more, 400+ (a rule of thumb; not checked against a source).

A light delivering ~300 µmol/m²/s for 16 h works out to a daily light integral of about 17 mol/m²/day, a good target for lettuce. Without good airflow, aim a little lower (13–14) to avoid tip burn.

The tower stands up to **180 cm (about 6 ft)** with all three pot sizes stacked, so you want light that covers its **height**, not just the top. A 1 m bar covers roughly half of a full-height column; use two per side, or build the tower shorter. A commenter built LED strips into the optional support rings. Options:

- **Full-spectrum LED bars or strips** mounted vertically alongside the tower, one per side. This is usually the best fit.
- **Full-spectrum LED panel or bar** above the seedling tray for the starting area.
- **T5 high-output fluorescents** — work well for seedlings, cheaper but bulkier and less efficient.

Look for a manufacturer-stated PPFD (or PAR) figure at a stated distance, not just "equivalent to 1000 W" claims. Give the light its **own timer** (14–16 h on). The pump runs its interval cycle around the clock on a **separate** timer.

## Heat

Unheated garage lows will drop the reservoir towards freezing, which stops growth and can split pumps and fittings.

1. **Insulate** — wrap or place the reservoir in foam board or a cooler-style enclosure; keep it off the cold concrete floor on a pallet or foam.
2. **Aquarium heater** (adjustable) in the reservoir, set about 68 °F, plugged into a GFCI. This is the single most useful heat item. It must stay **fully submerged**: a heater exposed by a falling water level gets very hot very fast.
3. **Freeze protection for everything else** — the reservoir heater only protects the tank. The tower column, tubing, net-cup roots, and pump all sit in garage air. If the forecast is near 32 °F, bring the tower inside or drain the column and lines, and keep the plants somewhere warm.
4. **Room-level heat** — insulation plus the reservoir heater is the preferred plan. If you add an electric space heater, plug it **directly into a wall outlet** (never a power strip or extension cord), keep 3 ft of clearance, use one with tip-over shutoff, and don't leave it running unattended; NFPA advises unplugging it when you leave. **No fuel-burning heaters** in the garage (carbon monoxide), and keep fuels and solvents away from any heater.
5. **Seedling heat mat** with a thermostat for germination.

## Timers and power

- **A timer for the lights** (14–16 h on) and a **separate timer for the pump** (15 min on, 15–45 off, around the clock) so the schedules are independent.
- **GFCI outlet or GFCI adapter** — not optional.
- **Surge-protected power strip** mounted above the water line (for the lights and timers, not for a heater).
- **Low-water protection** — as the tank drops, the pump can run dry (which damages it) and the heater can end up in air. Check the level daily; a float switch or low-water cutoff is cheap insurance.

## Meters and supplies

- **pH meter** (digital pen type) with calibration solution (pH 4 and 7); calibrate regularly and store as directed. Cheap pH test drops or strips work for rough checks only.
- **EC meter** — buy EC, not just TDS. TDS is calculated from EC with a 0.5 or 0.7 factor, so ppm readings from different meters don't match.
- **pH Down** (and pH Up) — small bottles last a long time.
- **Complete hydroponic nutrient** — a two- or three-part set designed for leafy greens.
- **Seed-starting tray, humidity dome, heat mat, rockwool or plugs** — see [Seed Starting](../seed-starting/).
- **Small clip-on fan** for airflow. Seedlings need it, and grown lettuce needs it too to prevent tip burn and damp mold, especially in a closed tent.
- **Measuring syringes or a graduated cylinder** for doses.
- **Opaque tubing and a lid** on the reservoir to keep out light and algae. Print or paint the tower parts opaque too (check the model for PETG vs PLA; PETG copes better with a cold, wet garage).

## Water and mixing

- **Know your tap water.** Check your utility's water-quality report. Very soft water (Asheville's runs about 24 ppm alkalinity) starts near zero EC and has little pH buffering, so pH swings fast, and some nutrient formulas need a Cal-Mag supplement. Chlorine is fine; chloramine is not removed by letting water sit. Well water needs a water analysis first.
- **Mixing:** add each nutrient part to the water **separately** and stir; never combine the concentrates. Measure EC first, then adjust pH. pH Down is acid: wear gloves and eye protection.
