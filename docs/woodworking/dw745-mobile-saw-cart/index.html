    DW745 Mobile Saw Cart (Proposed)

    DW745 Mobile Saw Cart (Proposed)

    A proposed build: a small plywood cart on locking casters for a DeWalt DW745 10" jobsite table saw. The cart is also a sealed dust cabinet for my Rockler 650 CFM dust collector. The saw sits on a gasket over a cutout in the top, so everything it drops falls into a sealed box. A steep ramp inside slides it into a full-width slotted pickup at the back. It’s deliberately compact: the footprint is just over the saw’s table, and the plywood comes out of one sheet. The saw lifts off with four knobs when it needs to go to a jobsite.

    • Footprint: 28" W × 24" D. The DW745’s table is about 630 × 570 mm (24.8" × 22.4", per a retailer listing), so the cart is ~1.6" bigger on each side and ~0.8" front and back.
    • Saw table height: 34" from the floor, a standard bench height, so a workbench can serve as outfeed. Some shops prefer ~35.5" (900 mm); pick yours and plug it into the formula below.
    • Dust: a sealed plenum with a ~37° ramp down to a slotted 4" PVC manifold. The manifold runs the full width of the back and exits the left side, so the cart can sit against a wall. The saw’s own rear port is hosed into the same box, and one hose runs to the collector.
    • Storage: an 8"-deep drawer under the ramp, outside the sealed space.
    • Casters: 4 of my step-stop locking casters, one at each corner, plus ~25 lb of ballast sealed in under the ramp.
    • Material: ¾" plywood, a ¼" hardboard ramp, and a 28" length of 4" PVC. Glue, screws, and caulk on every plenum seam.

    How the dust gets out

    Sealed plenum. The plenum is a closed box under the saw. Its walls are the top, sides, back, ramp, and the upper face of the manifold pipe. The only openings are:

    • the cutout under the saw’s open bottom, sealed to the saw base with foam weatherstrip;
    • a 2½" flange high in the back panel, which the saw’s rear port is hosed into;
    • the slot in the manifold.

    Every interior seam gets caulk, including the ramp-to-top joint at the ramp’s head and the ramp-to-pipe joint at its foot.

    Ramp. ¼" hardboard, its smooth face up, starts at the front edge of the cutout, since dust can’t fall anywhere ahead of it. It runs down to the pipe, landing just in front of the slot. That’s 11.1" of drop over 14.85" of run, about 37°. Sawdust’s angle of repose is roughly 35–45°, so steeper is better: a 17" box gives 39°. Wax the ramp.

    Slotted manifold, not a single port. A 4" port only pulls hard within about 4" of itself, and a 26½"-wide ramp would leave chips piled in the corners. Instead, a piece of 4" PVC sewer & drain pipe lies along the back-bottom corner, sealed against the back panel and bottom.

    • Slot: about 22" long, cut along the pipe facing front-up at roughly 45°, where the ramp delivers. Taper it from ½" wide at the exit end to ¾" at the closed end so the pull is even along its length. That’s about 13¾ in², similar to the 4" duct’s 12½ in².
    • Ends: the far end butts the right side panel, which caps it (caulk the joint). The other end passes through the left side panel and sticks out ~1½" for the collector hose.
    • Offcut guard: the slot is too narrow for an offcut to reach the single-stage collector’s impeller.

    Makeup air. A collector moves air, not dust. Air comes in through the saw’s blade slot, bevel slot and vents, sweeps down through the cutout, and leaves through the slot. Don’t seal those saw openings. If an incense test shows a weak draw at the blade throat, add a 1½" bleed hole with a sliding cover high in one side panel. A zero-clearance throat insert helps both suction and offcuts.

    The numbers.

    • Airflow: the collector is rated at 650 CFM free air. With a short hose and this box it should realistically move 350–450 CFM.
    • Duct speed: that’s 4,000–5,200 ft/min in the 4" line, above the ~3,500 ft/min that keeps chips moving in a duct.
    • Slot speed: about 3,700–4,700 ft/min at that flow. The plenum itself (~1.9 ft³) sees only a few hundred ft/min, so chips get to the slot by sliding down the ramp, not by being blown.
    • Hose: keep the hose to the collector short (≤ 10 ft) and smooth-walled.

    Rear port. The saw’s 2½" hose ends in the plenum, which is under suction, so it does draw some air. But it’s a weaker pull than a direct duct. If the incense test shows the blade shroud isn’t clearing, run that hose to a 4 × 4 × 2½" wye on the collector line instead.

    Set the height first

    Two measurements decide the carcass height. Take them before cutting anything:

    1. s: from the bottom of the saw’s base (where it’ll sit on the cart) to the top of the saw table.
    2. c: from the caster’s mounting plate to the floor.

    Carcass height H = 34" − s − c. That’s the outside height of the box, top and bottom panels included. The weatherstrip compresses to about ⅛", which is close enough to ignore.

    Example (yours will differ)scHInterior (H − 1½")Ramp angle
    3" casters13.5"3.5"17"15.5"~39°
    4" casters13.5"4.5"16"14.5"~37°
    5" casters13.5"5.5"15"13.5"~34°

    The s = 13.5" here is only a placeholder; I don’t have a published base-to-table height for the DW745. Measure your saw. Ramp angle = atan((interior − 3.4") ÷ 14.85"). The 3.4" is where the ramp lands on the pipe; 14.85" is the run from the cutout’s front edge to the pipe.

    Build

    The order matters. Three parts get sealed in early: the caster bolts, the top’s T-nuts, and the parts under the ramp.

    1. Size the cutout. Turn the saw over and check what’s open on its underside. Trace its base onto the top, then cut the hole ½″ inside the base outline so the base sits on solid wood all the way around. Note where the cutout’s front edge lands, because that’s where the ramp’s head goes.
    2. Bottom panel first: bolt the four caster plates on with the bolt heads inside. Counterbore the front pair so the drawer slides over them.
    3. Top panel: drill the saw’s four mounting holes and install the ¼″-20 T-nuts from underneath.
    4. Slot and fit the manifold. Cut the tapered slot in the PVC, either a series of overlapping holes cleaned up with a jigsaw, or a router guided by a fence. Bore the 4¼″ hole in the left side panel low at the back corner.
    5. Box: glue and screw the sides to the bottom. Slide the pipe through the left side and seat it in the back-bottom corner. Fit the back, then caulk the pipe to the back panel, the bottom, and the right side, which caps its end.
    6. Under the ramp: set the ballast in the space between the drawer bay and the pipe. Fit the drawer-bay back 10″ behind where the front panel goes, then the ramp cleats.
    7. Ramp: glue and screw the ramp onto the cleats, its foot just in front of the slot. Caulk all four edges and the foot-to-pipe joint.
    8. Front panel, then the top: fit the front panel. Caulk the top edges of every wall and the ramp head, then glue and screw the top on. Run weatherstrip around the cutout so the saw base presses down on it.
    9. Ports and hoses: install the 2½″ flange in the back. Set the saw on the gasket and knob it down. Connect its rear port to the flange, and connect the manifold stub to the collector.
    10. Smoke test: run the collector and hold a stick of incense around the seams. Caulk anything that draws. Then check the draw at the blade throat, and add the bleed hole or the wye if it’s weak.
    11. Drawer: build it slideless, wax its bottom edges and the bottom panel, and fit the pull.

    Using it safely

    • Lock all four casters before you turn the saw on. Point the swivels outward as you lock them, which widens the stance. Step-stop casters that lock both the wheel and the swivel are what you want. A cart that creeps during a rip is a kickback risk.
    • Mind the tipping point. Loaded and ballasted, the cart weighs about 120 lb. A push of roughly 37 lb front-to-back or 44 lb sideways at table height would tip it.
    • Support long stock. At 34″ a workbench or roller stand works as infeed and outfeed support. Don’t rip sheet goods without it.
    • Start the collector before the saw, and run it 10–15 seconds after the saw stops. That way there’s airflow before the dust arrives, and the plenum and hose clear afterward.
    • Offcuts: the slot stops them, but they’ll collect on the ramp. Unplug the saw, lift it off (four knobs), and clear them every so often. Never reach in through the blade opening.
    • Check your collector’s specs. Rockler lists its 650 CFM Dust Right wall-mount (TADC-34WN) as ¾ hp. Measure your inlet and size the manifold stub to match rather than adding a reducer.

    Buy instead?

    DeWalt’s DW7451 compact stand is listed for the DW745. It folds, it’s quick, and it’s often cheaper than this cart’s parts, but it has no dust control. It’s the better choice if the saw travels a lot. This cart wins in the shop: dust collection, storage, a solid base, and casters you already own.
