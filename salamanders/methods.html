How this page was made, and how to update it

The species page was compiled on 31 October 2026. This companion page records how the research was done, which source won when sources disagreed, what was synthesized rather than taken from a source, what could not be verified, and the exact steps for re-running each part when the science or the regulations change.

Process

The page was researched and built by Claude, an AI model from Anthropic, working in Claude Code under the site owner's direction. Claude used live web search and fetched primary sources. It did not draw on its own background knowledge for anything time-sensitive. Read the page with that in mind: every substantive claim traces to a cited source, but the synthesis has not been peer-reviewed.

  1. Three parallel research passes

    Three independent research agents ran at the same time, each with a narrow brief. Their prompts are reproduced in the workbook.

    • Taxonomy and species accounts: the current checklist, recent revisions, ranges, elevations, microhabitats, sizes, reproduction and ID notes.
    • Conservation and regulation: the NC protected list, NCNHP and NatureServe ranks, ESA and IUCN status, the hellbender listing history, threats, permits and decontamination.
    • Photographs: freely licensed images with full attribution.
    • Places (added 1 Nov 2026): DuPont State Recreational Forest, Transylvania County and the NC Blue Ridge Parkway. Covers documented species, habitat, rules, permits and post-Helene closures. This pass also corrected two earlier statements: the national-forest rock-collection rule and the D. marmoratus type locality.

    Each agent was told to cite a URL for every non-obvious claim and to mark anything it could not verify.

  2. Source reconciliation

    The reports were merged using the source hierarchy below. Where they conflicted, the regulatory text or the primary taxonomic paper won over secondary summaries. Items still flagged as unverified were dropped, softened to match what the source supports, or listed under Unverified.

  3. Synthesis

    The elevation chart, the mountain-block matrix and the microhabitat grouping are the compiler's synthesis of the species accounts, not values copied from any one source. See Judgement calls.

  4. Photo review

    Every candidate photo was reviewed by eye on a contact sheet. Photos were rejected when they did not show diagnostic features, such as a mudpuppy without visible gills, or when they suggested a different taxon, such as a Cheoah Bald candidate with red legs. An egg mass was kept only when captioned as an egg mass. Captions give the state only, never a finer locality.

  5. Render check

    The built page was screenshotted in headless Chrome at desktop and narrow widths, and the chart, matrix, catalog, dossier and tables were inspected before publishing.

Judgement calls

Synthesized, not sourced

  • Elevation bars are generalized for Western North Carolina from the species accounts, descriptions and regional literature. Where a description gave a range (e.g. P. shermani 853–1,494 m, P. cheoah 855–1,543 m, P. meridianus 543–823 m) it was used. Otherwise the bar is an informed generalization. Treat them as guidance, not range limits.
  • The mountain-block matrix uses 12 blocks drawn for this page. Presence is coded from county and range statements in the accounts. “Marginal” marks the edge of a range, a peripheral occurrence, or a range statement that does not clearly include the whole block.
  • Microhabitat groups put each species in its primary adult microhabitat along the stream-to-ridge gradient. Many species use more than one.
  • Forest-zone shading on the chart is approximate: about 1,000 m and 1,400 m as the boundaries between oak/cove hardwoods, northern hardwoods and spruce–fir. These boundaries vary with aspect and latitude.

Naming and scope decisions

  • Mudpuppy: kept as Necturus maculosus (SSAR, NCWRC) instead of N. louisianensis (ASW). The conflict is explained in the species account.
  • Crevice salamander: treated within Plethodon yonahlossee (Highton et al. 2012), with the NC Special Concern listing of the Hickory Nut Gorge population noted.
  • P. jacksoni is used for NC animals that NCWRC still lists as Wehrle's salamander.
  • Excluded pending vouchers: D. aureatus, D. conanti s.s., D. planiceps, D. kanawha, P. glutinosus s.s., A. jeffersonianum and A. talpoideum.
  • Photo IDs for cryptic Desmognathus rely on iNaturalist community identification. That is largely locality-based, so a photo illustrates the habitus, not a diagnosis.
  • Location data: the page gives no point localities or site directions. Ranges are stated at the level of mountain ranges, counties and drainages, and photo captions give only the state.

Could not be verified

These items were flagged during research. Where they touch the page, the wording is cautious. They are the first things to check on a re-run.

  • Post-revision count of NC salamander species. NCWRC has not published one; the estimated 72–76 is not used on the page.
  • County-level NC ranges for P. richmondi, P. jacksoni, D. aeneus and E. longicauda. For E. longicauda, sources disagree between the extreme west and the extreme northwest.
  • Whether D. mavrokoilius reaches Atlantic-slope drainages, and whether D. kanawha is in the NC New River.
  • Sizes given as “about” are standard field-guide values (Petranka 1998; Beane et al. 2010) that were not re-verified for each species after the revisions. The size of D. folkertsi is unverified.
  • The elevation range ASW gives for P. amplus (1,109–1,116 m) appears to be wrong, and its D. imitator range text is wrong.
  • IUCN categories were read through pages that cite the assessment DOIs. Junaluska (2004) and Weller's (2004) may have newer assessments.
  • Court activity in CBD v. USFWS, No. 4:26-cv-00093 (D. Ariz.) after the complaint was filed.
  • Whether the January 2025 second interim Lacey Act salamander rule has been finalized.
  • Effect sizes in Pugh et al. (2016) (paywalled), and the authors of the 2023 Freshwater Science TN/NC hellbender paper, which is therefore not cited.
  • Research and collection permit rules for the National Forests in North Carolina and for Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians lands.
  • No published herpetofaunal inventory exists for DuPont State Recreational Forest, and there are no site-level salamander lists for Richland Balsam, Graveyard Fields or Doughton Park. The Places section relies on iNaturalist counts for these.
  • Holotype vs. lectotype for D. marmoratus: Amphibian Species of the World was offline, so the page gives only the type locality.
  • 2026 status of the Pisgah Ranger District designated-camping order, the Gorges State Park backcountry and DuPont’s Grassy Creek bridge.
  • No source was found for a Zoo Knoxville hellbender head-starting program, “Hellbender Local” or a “Hellbender Day”; none is mentioned on the page.

Re-run workbook

Everything on the species page comes from three files in static/salamanders/, which are edited by hand. The photo steps are scripted.

Where things live

js/data.js
Families, microhabitats, basins, mountain blocks, forest zones, the 56 species records, and the reference list. Per-species fields: sci, auth, common, family, habitat, elev [min, max] in metres, basins, ranges (2 = present, 1 = marginal), range, microhabitat, size, repro, id_notes, notes, tax, formerly, endemic (“NC” or “SA”), nc (E/T/SC), fed/fedShort, grank, srank, iucn, statusNote, watch, photo.
index.html
All prose: hotspot, gradients, the hellbender section and listing timeline, threats, fieldwork.
assets/img/credits.json
Photo attribution; mirrored to js/credits.js by the photo tool.
tools/salamanders/photos.py
Fetches, reviews and installs photos (repo root; not deployed).

Publishing a change

  1. Edit js/data.js or index.html, and update the “as of” dates in the hero, status section and footer.
  2. Bump every ?v=YYYYMMDD query string in index.html and methods.html. CSS, JS and images get a one-year immutable browser cache, so without this, returning visitors keep the old files.
  3. Run make serve and check localhost:1313/salamanders/ at desktop and phone widths.
  4. Open a PR and wait for PR Checks and Link Check. Merging to main runs Deploy (S3, CloudFront invalidation, gh-pages).

Photos

From the repository root (requires Python 3 with Pillow):

# candidates for every species without a photo (NC first, then range-wide)
python3 -I tools/salamanders/photos.py fetch

# look at .salamander-photos/sheet*.jpg, then install picks by index
python3 -I tools/salamanders/photos.py install maculosus=2 lycos=0

# after hand-editing assets/img/credits.json
python3 -I tools/salamanders/photos.py credits

Only CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA photos are fetched. Before installing, check that the diagnostic features are visible and the taxon looks right. Pass --all to look for better photos for species that already have one. Only the mudpuppy has no photo as of this compile.

Re-running the research with an AI agent

The briefs below are condensed versions of the prompts used for the October 2026 compile. Run them in parallel with an agent that has web search. Tell it today's date, and require a source URL for every claim plus an explicit list of unverified items. Then diff the results against js/data.js instead of regenerating the page from scratch: the existing judgement calls are deliberate.

Brief 1: taxonomy and species accounts 
Build a structured brief on the salamanders of Western North Carolina (NC Blue Ridge
mountain counties). Audience: professional herpetologists. Today is <DATE>; verify with
web search, do not rely on memory for anything changed since ~2020.

1. Complete checklist under current taxonomy, by family. Verify and explain recent
   revisions (Desmognathus complexes: seal, black-bellied, shovel-nosed, Ocoee, dusky,
   pygmy; the Plethodon gray-cheeked complex; Aneides caryaensis vs A. aeneus). Flag where
   SSAR standard names differ from the primary literature or ASW.
2. Per species: name + authority, common name, WNC range (ranges/counties/drainages;
   Tennessee basin vs Atlantic slope), elevation range, microhabitat, adult size,
   reproductive mode, 1-2 field-relevant facts.
3. Ecological patterns: Desmognathus stream-to-upland gradient, Plethodon elevational
   segregation and Hairston's experiments, shermani x teyahalee hybrid zone, why the
   region is a global hotspot (with sourced numbers).
4. 15-25 key references with DOIs.
Cite a URL for every non-obvious claim; list everything you could not verify.
Compare against the current static/salamanders/js/data.js and report only differences.
Brief 2: conservation status, threats, field practice 
For the same WNC salamander fauna, as of <DATE>:
1. Status table: current NC legal status (15A NCAC 10I; find the latest effective version
   and any pending amendment), NCNHP S-rank and NatureServe G-rank (latest NCNHP list),
   ESA status, IUCN category, NC Wildlife Action Plan priority.
2. Eastern hellbender: full federal ESA history with Federal Register citations and dates
   (2019 finding, litigation, 2024 proposed rule, any final rule or court ruling since);
   NC distribution by basin; life history; threats; current NC programs; recent NC
   research; post-Hurricane Helene survey results.
3. Threats: Bsal detection status in North America and Lacey Act rule status; Bd;
   ranavirus; climate (including counter-evidence); collection; storms.
4. Field practice: NCWRC, NPS, State Parks, National Forests in NC and EBCI permits;
   current decontamination guidance; sensitive-location data handling; reporting.
Cite a URL for every claim; list everything you could not verify.
Report only what differs from the current page.
Brief 3: photographs 
Prefer running tools/salamanders/photos.py. If delegating instead: find one photo per
species from Wikimedia Commons or iNaturalist research-grade observations, licensed CC0,
CC BY or CC BY-SA only (no NC/ND). Prefer NC localities. Download ~1280px JPEGs under
400 KB; record title, page URL, artist, license and license URL, species, and a caption
with locality no finer than state. Do not open downloaded files except to check type;
the compiler reviews every image visually before use.

Corrections and re-run results are welcome as issues or pull requests on the site repository.
